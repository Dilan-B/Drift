The Problem With Every Other Screen Time App

Screen Time, One Sec, Opal, ScreenZen — they all do variations of the same thing: set a timer, show a reminder, maybe add some friction. They're speed bumps on the road to Instagram.

The fatal flaw? They trust you. "Did you really read for 20 minutes?" Sure, you tap yes. No one checks. The system collapses the first time you're bored and honest isn't convenient.

How Drift's AI Verification Works

When you complete a task in Drift, you don't just tap "done." The app's AI checks your work:

  • Text tasks — tell the AI what you did. It asks follow-up questions a pretender can't answer. "You said you studied calculus for 30 minutes — what topic? What was the hardest problem?"
  • Photo proof — snap a picture of your completed workout log, your handwritten notes, your clean kitchen, your finished drawing. The AI sees it and decides if it matches the task.
  • Calendar tasks — Drift imports your calendar events as tasks. When the meeting or class is over, verify it the same way.

It's not about catching liars. It's about making the "done" button feel earned. When you know someone — even an AI — is checking, you do the task properly instead of half-heartedly.

Built on Apple's Own Frameworks

Drift isn't a hacky workaround. It uses the same Apple Screen Time API (FamilyControls + ManagedSettings + DeviceActivity) that Apple's own Screen Time uses. This means:

  • Real app blocking — not a "close this app" notification you can ignore. The apps are actually shielded by iOS at the system level.
  • Background enforcement — even if you force-quit Drift, iOS keeps counting your usage and re-applies the shield when your earned time runs out.
  • Privacy-first — Drift never sees which apps you picked. Apple's opaque token system means your app choices stay between you and iOS.

Control Drift With Siri

Drift is built on App Intents — the framework Apple introduced to replace SiriKit and the same one powering the new Siri's ability to chain actions across apps. That means you can run Drift hands-free:

  • "Hey Siri, check my Drift balance" — Siri tells you how much screen time you have left, without opening the app.
  • "Hey Siri, create a task in Drift" — dictate the task and Drift opens with it ready to go.
  • "Hey Siri, start a Drift In session" — begin a focus block by voice.
  • "Hey Siri, how am I doing in Drift?" — get today's progress read back to you.

Because these are registered as App Intents rather than one-off shortcuts, the rebuilt Siri can also fold them into multi-step requests — "start a focus session and put my phone on Do Not Disturb" works as a single instruction. They show up automatically in the Shortcuts app too, so you can wire Drift into your own automations: start a session when you arrive at the library, check your balance every morning, whatever fits.

No setup required. The voice commands work the moment you install Drift — no permissions to grant, no shortcuts to configure.

Apple Intelligence brings on-device AI to summarize, rewrite, and create. Drift adds a piece Apple left out: AI that holds you accountable.

The AI is server-side, not on-device

Drift's task verification runs through server-side AI (not Apple Intelligence). This is deliberate — server-side processing means the verification model can be updated instantly without an app update, and it works identically on every iPhone regardless of whether it supports Apple Intelligence.

Siri cannot control Apple's built-in Screen Time, but Drift registers its actions with Siri through App Intents. You can say "Hey Siri, check my Drift balance", "create a task in Drift", or "start a Drift In session" without opening the app. No setup or permissions are required.

What is the best AI screen time app for iPhone?

Drift is the only screen time app that uses AI to verify you actually completed a task before unlocking your apps. Other apps rely on timers and self-reporting, which are trivial to override. Drift asks follow-up questions or checks photo proof, then unlocks the time you earned.

How is Drift different from Apple Screen Time?

Apple Screen Time sets limits you can dismiss with one tap. Drift blocks your chosen apps until you earn time by completing a verified task, with no override button. Both use the same system-level blocking, but Drift replaces restriction with an earning model.

Does Drift work when the app is closed?

Yes. Drift uses Apple's DeviceActivity framework, so iOS keeps counting your usage and re-applies the block when your earned time runs out, even if Drift has been force-quit.
